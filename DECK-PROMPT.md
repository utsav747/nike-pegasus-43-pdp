# Deck prompt

The slide-by-slide prompt that produces the Pegasus 43 launch deck (12 slides), in the green-glow style.

---

Make a 12-slide, 16:9 launch deck for the Nike Pegasus 43 mobile product page (live at https://nike-pegasus-43-pdp.vercel.app, code at https://github.com/utsav747/nike-pegasus-43-pdp). Take every number from the repo (NOTES.md, PERFORMANCE-NOTES.md, the components and data/pegasus-43-stock.json) and invent none. The punch line "No pinch. No jump. Right size." opens and closes the main story.

## Style

Follow the look of a sports-brand impact report page:
- Background: white, with one large soft volt-green glow (core about #70F03A, fading through mint #D3F0DC to white). Move the glow to a different spot on each slide, and make it strongest behind the title and closing slides.
- Display type: Anton (heavy, condensed, uppercase) for titles, big numbers and step numbers. Titles are 26 to 44pt and tell the story in one sentence. Big numbers are 50 to 150pt.
- Body: Inter, 14 to 18pt, near-black #111111.
- Captions: Playfair Display italic, sitting under the big numbers and visuals.
- Icons: dot-matrix clusters of black circles, one per big stat, each shaped like its idea (a 7x4 grid for the size grid, a solid square for "holds still", a check, an arrow, a target, a split).
- Accent: volt green fills only the one element that matters on a slide (the outcome boxes, the size grid block, the selected step). Black cards with white text for emphasis.
- Chrome on every slide: "PEGASUS 43" in Anton top left; a section nav across the top (The moment, The problem, The fix, Next, Appendix) with the current section bold and marked with a dot; a breadcrumb under it ("The fix" plus an italic subtitle); a thin black rule at the bottom with the page number on the left and "Nike Pegasus 43 launch page" in the middle.
- Flat: no shadows, no gradients other than the glow, no accent lines under titles.

## Slides

1. **Title** (no section). Kicker "NIKE PEGASUS 43 · LAUNCH PRODUCT PAGE". Headline on three lines, very large: "NO PINCH. / NO JUMP. / RIGHT SIZE." Italic line: "A mobile-first product page, live now, built so a runner on a phone picks the right size on the first tap." On the right, a black phone frame holding the real 375px screenshot of the live page, caption "Live page at 375px phone width".
2. **The moment.** Title: "A runner on a phone has one job on this page: tap the right size." Four steps as huge numbers 01 to 04, each with a bold label above and an italic caption below: Hears about the launch (taps a link from a post, email or app); Opens the page on a phone (one hand, often on the move); Taps a size (the moment that decides the sale), shown on a black card with the label in volt; Adds to cart (only works if step 3 was right). Arrows between steps. Bottom line in bold: "If the size tap goes wrong, nothing after it matters: the runner either gives up or orders a shoe that does not fit."
3. **The problem.** Title: "Today's pattern: a desktop page squeezed onto a small screen." Left: a desktop page wireframe (image block, text lines, a 4x3 size grid), caption "Designed for a desktop screen". Arrow to a phone showing the same page shrunk, with a translucent green thumb circle covering several tiny size boxes, caption "Tiny targets, so the runner pinch-zooms". Right: "What the runner gets": everything shrinks to fit the screen; size buttons become targets smaller than a thumb; pinch, zoom and scroll sideways just to tap a size.
4. **The problem.** Title: "While the page loads, the grid jumps and the tap lands on the wrong size." A phone with a 4x3 grid (7 to 12.5). A ring marks where the thumb is aiming (size 9). A green "Late content" block appears above the grid and pushes it down, so the ring now sits on size 7 and turns red. Caption: "Still loading: thumb aims at size 9" then "A split second later: grid drops, tap hits size 7". Right: "Why it goes wrong": content arrives late and pushes the size grid down; the thumb is already on its way, so it lands on the row above; the wrong size is selected, often without the runner noticing.
5. **The problem.** Title: "A wrong tap ends in one of two costs: an abandoned cart or a return." Flow: a black box "Wrong size tapped" branches to two rows. Top: Runner notices, has to fix it on a tiny screen, then Frustrated, gives up, then (green) Abandoned cart, lost sale. Bottom: Runner does not notice, order ships, then Shoe does not fit, then (green) Return: shipping, handling, unhappy runner. Italic note: "We have not measured these costs for this page yet. The next steps slide proposes how."
6. **The fix.** Title: "The fix: a size grid built for the thumb, designed at phone width first." Four size states as real buttons: 9 Available, 9.5 Selling out fast (note inside the button), 10 Sold out (light grey), 8 Selected (black fill). Big numbers from the 8 December snapshot: 26 sizes, 10 in stock, 7 selling out fast, 9 sold out, with the source line "Real stock data, 8 December snapshot". Right: dot-grid icon and a huge "44PX" under "Every size button at 375px is a comfortable thumb target", italic caption: "Designed at 375px phone width first, not shrunk from desktop. Narrower phones scale gently: about 43px at 360px, 38px at 320px. Sold-out sizes cannot be tapped."
7. **The fix: the layout plan.** Title: "Every block has a fixed height, so the size grid always starts at 400px." Diagram: a phone-shaped stack of six blocks drawn to scale at 375px wide, in reading order, top to bottom: 01 Image 300px (grey, "PHOTO"), 02 Title 50px, 03 Price 50px, 04 Size grid 220px (volt green, with the 7, 7, 7, 5 grid of 44px squares drawn inside to scale), 05 Tech copy 300px, 06 Reviews 300px. Each block has a leader line to its label (number, name, height in Anton). A ruler on the left marks 0px page top, 400px grid top (bold), 812px first screen ends (dashed line across the phone; the phone frame covers the first 812px) and 1,220px page end. Bullets: six blocks in reading order, each with a fixed height in code; planned 1,220px, measured 1,220px at 375px width; tech copy and reviews stay 300px until tapped open. Source: NOTES.md, section heights measured with Playwright at 375px. Speaker notes: a page jumps when content arrives late and the browser makes room for it, pushing everything below down. Here every box reserves its height before its content arrives (the photo box is 300px whether the photo has loaded or not), the CSS is inlined so the heights apply on first paint, and no web font is downloaded, so nothing above the grid can grow and nothing can push it.
8. **The fix: proof.** Centre headline "IT HOLDS STILL" with the line "The size grid does not move while the page loads, even on a slow connection." Around it, five big stats, each with a bold label above, a dot icon, and an italic caption: 0PX size grid movement during load (top edge stays at 400px from first paint); 0 layout shift score; 99 Lighthouse performance score (live site, Slow 4G phone profile); 1.62S largest paint, live site on Slow 4G (limit 2.5s); 123MS total blocking time (limit 200ms). Bottom centre: "Checked automatically after every Vercel deploy, via GitHub." with links to the live Lighthouse report and PERFORMANCE-NOTES.md.
9. **Next.** Title: "Next steps: prove the business impact and fix one copy detail." Two columns with huge 01 and 02 and a dot icon each: Measure the business impact (run an A/B test of the old and new page on wrong-size returns and cart abandonment; today the impact is a hypothesis, with no conversion data yet). Confirm the colorway text (the product photo shows a grey-blue shoe, but the copy says Black/White-Volt; align them before launch).
10. **Next.** Title: "How we built it." Five columns under thin top rules, each with an Anton number, a bold name and an italic role: Next.js, React and Tailwind (Page); GitHub (Code); Vercel (Hosting and live deploys); Lighthouse CI and GitHub Actions (Automated checks); Claude (Building and iterating). Then "CHECKS RUN ON EVERY DEPLOY." in Anton and the line "Every push to main and every Vercel production deploy runs Lighthouse CI and the grid-movement check on GitHub Actions."
11. **Close.** Centred over the strongest glow: "LIVE NOW", then "NO PINCH. NO JUMP. RIGHT SIZE." on one line, the link nike-pegasus-43-pdp.vercel.app, and the italic line "Built for the thumb · Holds still while it loads · Right size on the first tap".
12. **Appendix.** Title: "Lighthouse results: local build vs live site." Table (bold header, row lines only, numbers right-aligned): Performance score, at least 90, 100, 99; Largest paint, under 2.5 s, 1.74 s, 1.62 s; Blocking time, under 200 ms, 66 ms, 123 ms; First paint, under 1.8 s, 0.64 s, 0.82 s; Speed index, under 3.4 s, 0.64 s, 0.83 s; Layout shift, 0, 0, 0; Grid movement, 0px, 0px, 0px. Black card on the right: "BOTH PASS EVERY LIMIT" in volt, then "Blocking time is higher live because the page now decodes the real 33 KB product photo. The grid still never moves." Footnote: median of 3 runs on Slow 4G (375x812 phone, 150 ms round trip, 1.6 Mbps, 4x slower CPU); local was next start on a MacBook with the placeholder image; live was Vercel with the real photo; both 18 Nov 2026. Links to the full live report and PERFORMANCE-NOTES.md.

Keep the speaker notes for every slide: say what the slide shows and why it matters, in the presenter's voice.

## Motion (HTML version)

Build the same deck as one HTML page: a 1600x900 stage scaled to the window, with arrow keys, click and swipe to move, a progress bar and a link per slide (#slide-7). Motion:
- The glow drifts slowly and glides to its new spot on each slide change.
- Slide content rises in with a short stagger. The title headline slides up line by line.
- Slide 4 loops: the late content grows, the grid drops, and the ring turns red on the wrong size.
- Slide 7 stacks the blocks one by one in reading order, then draws the leader lines and labels, and the size grid block pulses green.
- Big numbers count up. "0PX" shakes once and settles still.
- The closing punch line lands one phrase at a time.
- Respect reduced motion: show every slide in its final state with no animation.
